La Veine Bleue

8 × 52 min · Geopolitical thriller · Moral drama · Premium series

Sent to audit a powerful mining group, an Ivorian lawyer discovers the human and political cost hidden behind its compliance reports — and must decide how much of the system she is willing to destroy to expose the truth.

SeriesResources · Power · Truth

Logline

Amara Diop has built her career inside systems that know how to turn responsibility into procedure. Sent to audit a powerful mining group, she discovers contradictions between corporate reports, local realities and political interests. The deeper she goes, the more the audit stops being a professional assignment and becomes a choice about what she is willing to risk to expose the truth.

The story

Amara is not an outsider looking into an opaque industry. She understands its language, its procedures and the professional logic that makes complex systems appear controllable. That access is precisely what gives her investigation its power — and what makes her vulnerable.

What begins as a difficult audit gradually exposes a network in which corporate reputation, strategic minerals, political influence and human consequences are inseparable. Amara must move from documenting contradictions to deciding what they demand of her.

The world

La Veine Bleue enters the world of strategic minerals through the people whose job is to make an opaque system look clean. Corporate offices, mine sites, communities and political networks are connected by decisions whose consequences rarely remain where they were made.

The cobalt pressure point

The cobalt economy is not background decoration. It is the pressure point where money, technology, sovereignty, reputation and human lives meet. The thriller grows from that collision rather than treating geopolitics as exposition.

The dramatic engine

Every revelation changes the cost of the next decision. Amara's professional position gives her access, but that same position can be turned against her. The investigation therefore operates on two levels: the immediate suspense of uncovering what the reports conceal, and the longer game of power around resources and political survival.

The central question

Who is responsible is only the beginning. The deeper question is what happens when telling the truth threatens the structure that gave you access to it — and when neutrality itself becomes a choice.

Amara Diop

Amara's strength is that she knows how the system speaks. Her arc moves from professional compliance toward personal responsibility: from believing that systems can be corrected from within to confronting what she is prepared to risk when correction is no longer enough.

Series ambition

The first season is conceived as a complete investigative and moral arc while opening a larger world capable of sustaining further stories. The ambition is a contemporary thriller rooted in African realities while connected to global stakes.
