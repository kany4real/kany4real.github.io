Une Clé à Conakry

FEATURE FILM · URBAN FAMILY DRAMEDY · IN DEVELOPMENT

“He wanted a room of his own. He will have to learn what a key really opens.”

Feature filmFamilyFreedomConakry

Logline

In Conakry, a young man from a Congolese family pretends to be engaged so he can rent a room of his own, only to discover that freedom costs more than rent.

The story

Henry, 19, wants something that looks simple: a place of his own and the right to make decisions like an adult. But finding a room in Conakry is not only a financial problem. It is a question of trust, reputation, guarantees and the people who believe they have a say in his life.

So Henry creates a shortcut. He invents an engagement. The lie gives him the credibility he needs to open a door, but it also creates a social situation that quickly becomes harder to control. A private attempt at independence turns into a web involving his family, a young woman and the neighbourhood around them.

The city

Conakry is not a postcard backdrop. The city becomes part of the pressure system: housing, movement, family proximity, neighbourhood reputation and the practical negotiations of everyday life constantly shape Henry’s choices. The film is interested in the humour and friction that emerge when private ambitions collide with the collective structures around them.

The dramatic engine

The lie keeps generating consequences. Every attempt to make the fake engagement believable creates a new obligation, a new misunderstanding or a new person who now has an interest in the story being true.

Henry

Henry is not trying to reject his family or his culture. He is trying to become an adult inside a world where adulthood is negotiated collectively. His problem is that he wants the privileges of independence before he has learned the cost of the freedom he is asking for.

Why this film

Une Clé à Conakry uses a simple social problem — finding a room — to tell a broader story about autonomy, family expectations, masculinity, reputation and the comic contradictions of becoming your own person.
