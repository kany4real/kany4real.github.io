Sortie de Route

8 × 52 min · Supernatural thriller · African tech-noir · Original series

In a hyperconnected city, a ride-hailing driver and a disgraced inspector are drawn into a chain of impossible deaths where reflections, screens and an unseen digital presence begin to rewrite reality.

The story

Ajara makes a living behind the wheel, navigating a city where movement, money and reputation are increasingly mediated by the MOVE app. When a death that should be impossible refuses to stay buried, she crosses paths with Kouakou, a disgraced inspector who has his own reasons for distrusting the official version of events.

The investigation opens onto a hidden layer of the city: glitches that repeat, reflections that behave independently, traces that disappear and a phenomenon connected to the N'DRI lineage. What first looks like a sequence of accidents gradually becomes a fight over who gets to control what is real.

The world

Sortie de Route turns ordinary urban infrastructure into a source of unease: ride-hailing, phones, CCTV, screens, mirrors, traffic and algorithmic systems become possible points of entry for something that should not be there.

The series is rooted in African urban reality rather than using technology as decoration. Its supernatural mythology grows through family history, social memory and the N'DRI lineage, while the emerging phenomenon known as Red Noise gives the season a larger mystery beyond the first investigation.

The dramatic engine

Each episode advances two investigations at once: the immediate mystery of a new anomaly or death, and the deeper question of what is happening to the city itself. Personal secrets, fractured relationships and conflicting versions of the truth continually collide with the supernatural investigation.

The season is designed around progressive revelations: what begins as an isolated disturbance becomes evidence of a system, then a lineage, and finally a threat that forces the characters to question the reality they thought they understood.

Key characters

Ajara is practical, mobile and difficult to intimidate. Her position behind the wheel puts her at the intersection of the city's hidden movements and makes her an unwilling witness.

Kouakou, a disgraced inspector, brings investigative instincts and institutional baggage to the partnership. Their alliance is useful precisely because neither fully trusts the other.

Around them, family histories and personal loyalties — including the legacy of N'DRI and the absence left by Koffi — turn the supernatural mystery into something intimate.
