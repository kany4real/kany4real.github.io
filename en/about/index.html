The author

Writing for the screen, thinking from the frame.

I write about people pushed into choices they cannot undo.

My stories begin with pressure: a character wants something, the world resists, and every decision carries a cost. Genre becomes the engine — supernatural thriller, geopolitical drama, family dramedy, social thriller — but character remains the point of entry.

I come from the Democratic Republic of the Congo and have lived and worked across Central and West African contexts. That trajectory feeds a pan-African perspective attentive to cities, movement, power, memory and the intimate consequences of collective pressure.

“I think about mise-en-scène from the page onward: rhythm, atmosphere, space, silence and point of view are all part of my writing process.”

As a writer-director, I move between script, set and edit. I want the dramatic idea and the visual language to grow together rather than being treated as separate stages of the work.

The ambition is simple: make African realities specific on screen without making them inaccessible — stories rooted in where they come from and able to travel.

Creative practice

Three things I bring into the room.

01 · Story

Dramatic pressure

Character, conflict and consequence come first. The premise has to generate scenes, choices and escalation.

02 · Direction

From script to frame

Performance, blocking, atmosphere, camera and rhythm are considered from the writing stage onward.

03 · Territory

Rooted, not closed

I build stories from African realities while aiming for themes, genres and emotions that travel.
